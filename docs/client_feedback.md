# Client Feedback and Redesign Brief
## North Woods Coffee Company

**Website under review:** https://husson-webdesign.github.io/brooks-P1-NorthwoodsCoffee/

Hello,

Thank you for sharing the first version of our website. I appreciate the warm introduction to our café, the Maine-inspired drinks and bakery items, and the detailed catering options. The descriptions give visitors a good starting point for imagining what we offer. The green and natural colors also suggest a direction that could work well for our brand.

For the next version, I would like a much more complete and polished redesign. A customer should be able to experience our café's personality, browse the menu comfortably, find practical visiting information, and explore catering without searching through a long page. Right now, all the main content appears on one page, the opening image did not load when I viewed the site, and the presentation does not yet fully capture the warm, inviting character I want for North Woods Coffee Company.

## Phase 1: Define Our Brand and Message

### 1. Create a memorable café identity

Please build on the natural green palette to create a distinctive identity inspired by coffee, Maine, and a welcoming neighborhood gathering place. I would like:

- A polished wordmark or simple logo using the approved business name.
- A coordinated color palette, readable typography, and consistent image style.
- A welcoming tone that feels appropriate for students, families, professionals, and visitors.
- Consistent buttons, cards, spacing, and page headings.

The design should feel warm and professional across every page, while remaining easy to read on a phone.

### 2. Strengthen the first impression

“Great Coffee, Maine Inspired” is a promising message. Please develop the opening so visitors can quickly understand that we are a café in Bangor offering coffee, specialty drinks, bakery items, and catering.

Use a shorter supporting introduction and clear choices such as **Explore the Menu** and **Plan a Visit**. Do not imply that online ordering is available unless it has been approved and actually works.

### 3. Tell our story with care

The current About section describes small-batch roasting, community involvement, and efforts to reduce waste. Please turn these ideas into a clear, personal story that explains what makes our café special.

Use the project brief to confirm statements about roasting, suppliers, community support, sustainability, and food preparation. Ask me about any missing details; do not invent a founding date, awards, certifications, partnerships, or customer reviews.

## Phase 2: Create a Complete Multipage Website

### 4. Give important information its own page

The current navigation moves between sections of one long page. Please redesign the site with separate, complete pages so customers can find information more easily. I would like at least:

1. **Home** — Introduce the café, highlight a few offerings, and guide visitors to the next step.
2. **About** — Share our story, approach to coffee, and place in the community.
3. **Menu** — Present coffee, specialty drinks, and bakery items clearly.
4. **Catering** — Explain the available services and how a customer can prepare an inquiry.
5. **Contact / Visit** — Show approved visiting details, hours, and the demonstration inquiry form.

Each page should have a clear purpose, a consistent navigation menu, and a useful next step. Visitors should also be able to open each page directly.

### 5. Make the homepage a useful introduction

Please include a strong opening image and message, a short introduction to the café, a preview of featured menu items, and a brief introduction to catering. Add a clear invitation to view the full menu or plan a visit.

The homepage should provide a taste of the café without repeating every detail from the other pages.

### 6. Make the About page more engaging

Organize our story into clear sections, such as our approach to coffee, the café experience, and community involvement. Use images or visual details to make the page inviting, and provide a link to the menu or visiting information.

Please verify our claims before presenting them as established facts. If more information is needed, list questions for me.

## Phase 3: Improve Visual Design and Layout

### 7. Fix the missing homepage image

When I viewed the homepage, the image did not appear and only the description “Customers seated inside the local coffee shop” was visible. Please fix or replace it and verify that it loads on the published website.

Choose an image that helps visitors picture a welcoming café. If the image does not show our actual café, do not present it as a photograph of our premises unless that is confirmed.

### 8. Create a warmer, more polished presentation

Please give the website a clearer visual hierarchy and a more balanced layout. Use a consistent content width, comfortable spacing, attractive sections, and legible text. Headings, prices, descriptions, and buttons should be easy to distinguish.

Use relevant coffee and bakery imagery where it helps visitors, but avoid overcrowding the page or making images distract from the menu information.

### 9. Use images responsibly

Select clear images that fit their spaces without stretching and can be used for this project. Document image sources and provide useful descriptions where appropriate.

If photos are illustrative rather than our actual products or café, present them honestly. Do not invent customer testimonials or show stock images as evidence of our own events.

### 10. Build a useful footer

The current footer contains only a copyright line. Please include the approved business name, key page links, visiting information, hours, and approved contact details where appropriate. Make the footer readable and useful on small screens.

## Phase 4: Make the Menu Easy to Explore

### 11. Give the menu a clear structure

The current menu already contains featured items, coffee, specialty drinks, and bakery choices. Please preserve the useful descriptions and prices, while making the menu easier to scan and compare.

For each item, show its name, description, and approved price consistently. Use clear category headings and make featured items easy to identify. Because the featured latte, cold brew, and scone are also shown in their categories, ensure their descriptions and prices stay consistent wherever they appear.

### 12. Help customers find what they want

Please add a simple way to browse by category, such as **All**, **Coffee**, **Specialty Drinks**, and **Bakery**. A search option may also be useful. Customers should be able to return to the full menu easily and receive a friendly message if no item matches.

Keep the menu readable and usable even for someone who does not use the filter.

### 13. Clarify information that needs approval

Please confirm prices, portion sizes, availability, ingredients, and claims such as “house-baked” or “baked fresh each morning” against the project brief. If details are missing, ask me rather than inventing them.

If dietary or allergen information is included, use only verified information. Do not label an item as safe for a specific allergy or diet without confirmation.

## Phase 5: Present Catering and Plan a Visit

### 14. Develop the catering page

The existing Office Coffee Service, Pastry Trays, and Event Beverage Station descriptions and starting prices are a useful foundation. Please make these offerings easy to compare and explain what information a customer should prepare, such as the type of event and approximate number of guests.

Retain “starting at” language where appropriate. Confirm prices, quantities, delivery, setup, lead times, and availability before making promises. Provide a clear route to the demonstration inquiry form, with the selected service carried over if possible.

### 15. Make visiting information easy to find

The website lists business hours in the About section, while the Contact section invites people to visit Bangor but does not show a street address or hours alongside the form. Please create a dedicated Visit or Contact area where visitors can find the approved location, hours, and contact details together.

Ask me to confirm the address, phone, email, hours, and any holiday schedule. If these details are fictional for the class project, identify them as sample information. Do not invent a street address or a map location.

## Phase 6: Inquiry Form and Interactive Features

### 16. Make the contact form an honest demonstration

The current form has name, email, and message fields and a **“Send Message”** button. For this class project, I understand that GitHub Pages will not send or save messages. Please make that clear before visitors fill out the form.

Rename the action to something accurate, such as **“Preview Inquiry.”** Check required fields and show clear guidance when information is missing. After the visitor completes the form, display a readable preview and allow changes. Do not say that a message was sent, a catering request received, or a reservation confirmed.

For catering inquiries, consider allowing visitors to choose the service and enter an event date or approximate guest count. Do not ask for more personal information than the demonstration needs.

### 17. Connect the customer journey

Make it easy to move from a featured item to the full menu, from catering information to the inquiry demonstration, and from the homepage to visiting details. If a catering service is selected, carry that choice to the form where possible and let the visitor change it.

Do not imply that a customer can place or pay for an order through the website unless such a feature has been approved and implemented.

## Phase 7: Make Menu Information Easier to Maintain

### 18. Organize the menu in JSON

I would like a future developer to be able to update menu items and prices without having to search through every page. Please keep the approved menu information in a separate **JSON file** and use it to display the menu on the site.

Please make sure:

- All approved coffee, specialty drink, and bakery items are included.
- Each item has a consistent name, category, description, and approved price.
- Featured items and their regular menu entries display consistent information.
- The category filter or search uses the same information.
- Visitors see a helpful message if the menu cannot be displayed.
- The project documentation explains where to find and update the information.

If time permits, organize the approved catering offerings in a separate JSON file as well. Please do not invent items, prices, availability, or dietary claims.

## Phase 8: Navigation, Accessibility, and Mobile Experience

### 19. Make the site easy to use on every device

Please test the redesigned pages on phones, tablets, and desktops. Text should be readable without zooming, images and menu cards should fit the screen, buttons should be easy to tap, and visitors should not need to scroll sideways.

Keep navigation consistent across all pages, show which page is active, and make the logo return to the homepage. If a mobile menu is needed, make it easy to open, close, and use with a keyboard.

### 20. Make the experience accessible

Check that visitors can navigate with a keyboard, see where they are focused, read text against its background, and understand form labels and error messages. Images should have appropriate descriptions. Search, filters, and other interactive controls should be usable without a mouse.

## Phase 9: Final Review and Handoff

### 21. Review the published website

Before presenting the redesign, please check that:

- Every page opens directly and can be reached through the navigation.
- The approved business name and visiting details are consistent.
- Images, links, buttons, menu filters, and catering choices work.
- The missing homepage image is fixed.
- Menu and catering information match the approved project details.
- The form clearly states that it is a demonstration and does not send or save information.
- JSON-powered content loads on GitHub Pages.
- The site works on phone, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, text readability, and image descriptions are reviewed.
- Spelling, grammar, and page titles are checked.

### 22. Provide a professional handoff

Please provide the published website link, project repository link, a short explanation of your changes, desktop and phone screenshots, a list of information needing my confirmation, image sources, known limitations, and instructions explaining how a future developer can update the menu and catering information.
